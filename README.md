# Optical Technology for AI Processing

Can light do the arithmetic of AI? A map of optical computing approaches and what each has shown; the price of an optical matrix multiply in conversions, precision and static power; where optics could win and where it cannot; and the public landscape of companies, with every company claim attributed to its source and labelled as the company's own. Fourier optics, training and device physics come from [Fourier Optics for Inference](https://github.com/BrendanJamesLynskey/LLM_Hub_Fourier_Optics_Inference), [Fourier Optics for Training](https://github.com/BrendanJamesLynskey/LLM_Hub_Fourier_Optics_Training) and [Silicon Photonics Fundamentals](https://github.com/BrendanJamesLynskey/Optical_Hub_Silicon_Photonics) and are linked, not repeated.

**Live index:** https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/Optical_Hub_AI_Processing/

## Where the numbers come from

Every number is cited on its slide to a paper, datasheet or public company source, with a link, or computed live by a deck's interactive:

- **Deck 01** simulates a rectangular Mach–Zehnder mesh with phase, splitter and loss errors and reports the output error; the error values are inputs, not measurements.
- **Deck 02** computes energy per MAC of an optical matrix–vector engine: converters at Walden FoM × 2^ENOB shared by N MACs, the shot-noise photon floor, weight loads and static power. Converter FoMs, laser efficiency, loss and static power are **illustrative**; the digital reference default is Horowitz's 45 nm 8-bit MAC.
- **Deck 03** maps the same model over matrix size and precision against the digital reference.
- **Deck 04** converts a claimed throughput and power to energy per MAC and compares it with the conversion floor; its defaults are Lightmatter's published figures, with the precision and converter FoM **illustrative**.

Company figures are the companies' own, quoted from their public sources and labelled as such; peer-reviewed results are marked; projections are called projections.

## Glossary: concepts and where they are explained

The same glossary, with anchors each deck links to, is on the [live index](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/Optical_Hub_AI_Processing/#glossary).

| Concept | What it is | Where it is explained |
|---|---|---|
| Optical computing (for AI) | Light doing part of a neural network's arithmetic, usually matrix–vector products, with electronics converting data in and out, applying nonlinearities and storing results. Distinct from optical interconnect, which only moves bits. | [OptAI 01 · what this series means by optical computing](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_01_Optical_Computing_Map/#slide-01); [OptAI 01 · what is not optical computing here](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_01_Optical_Computing_Map/#slide-12) |
| Interference, propagation and square-law detection | The three facts optical computing rests on: coherent fields add as complex amplitudes (a weighted sum), propagation through optics applies a fixed linear transform, and detectors measure power, losing sign and phase unless a reference is mixed in. | [OptAI 01 · three physical facts](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_01_Optical_Computing_Map/#slide-02) |
| Diffractive networks | Stacks of engineered diffractive layers whose pixels are the weights; light passing through computes a fixed linear network. Strongest on vision, where the input is already light: ACCEL and Taichi report peta-operation and 160 TOPS/W figures by their own definitions. | [OptAI 01 · diffractive networks](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_01_Optical_Computing_Map/#slide-05); [OptAI 03 · when the data is already light](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_03_Where_Optics_Wins/#slide-09) |
| MZI-mesh neural network | A programmable grid of Mach–Zehnder interferometers applying a matrix to coherent light: N(N−1)/2 MZIs per unitary, each path crossing about N of them, so errors and loss compound with size. Shen et al. ran a vowel classifier on 56 MZIs at 76.7% against 91.7% simulated. | [OptAI 01 · coherent MZI meshes](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_01_Optical_Computing_Map/#slide-06); [OptAI 01 · interactive mesh errors](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_01_Optical_Computing_Map/#slide-07); [OptAI 02 · scaling the array](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_02_Optical_Matrix_Multiply/#slide-09) |
| Incoherent WDM crossbar | One input per wavelength, weighted by attenuators, rings or phase-change cells and summed on a photodiode: robust to phase but needing many wavelengths and two detectors for signed values. The photonic tensor core of Feldmann et al. is the best-known example. | [OptAI 01 · incoherent and wavelength-multiplexed engines](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_01_Optical_Computing_Map/#slide-08) |
| Photoelectric multiplication | Encoding both operands in light and multiplying them by interference on a balanced detector, with time integration summing the products. It allows very large networks and a shot-noise floor that can be as low as 50 zJ per MAC in theory. | [OptAI 01 · photoelectric multiplication](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_01_Optical_Computing_Map/#slide-09); [OptAI 02 · the photon floor](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_02_Optical_Matrix_Multiply/#slide-05) |
| The nonlinearity problem | Optics is linear at these intensities, so activations are usually applied electronically between optical layers, at a conversion cost per layer. On-chip optoelectronic nonlinearities have run small deep networks in 410–570 ps. | [OptAI 01 · the nonlinearity problem](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_01_Optical_Computing_Map/#slide-10); [OptAI 03 · latency niches](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_03_Where_Optics_Wins/#slide-08) |
| Reuse: MACs per conversion | An N × N matrix–vector product is N2 MACs for 2N conversions, so each conversion is shared by N/2 MACs and converter energy per MAC falls as 1/N. Weights that change for every vector lose this reuse. | [OptAI 02 · the operation and its reuse](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_02_Optical_Matrix_Multiply/#slide-01); [OptAI 03 · the conditions for a win](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_03_Where_Optics_Wins/#slide-02) |
| Weight storage in optics | How an optical array holds its weights: thermo-optic phase shifters (continuous heater power), phase-change cells (non-volatile, few levels, slow writes), electro-optic shifters, or modulator pixels. Static power per weight is paid at every use. | [OptAI 02 · storing the weights](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_02_Optical_Matrix_Multiply/#slide-03); [OptAI 02 · static power and utilisation](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_02_Optical_Matrix_Multiply/#slide-08) |
| The photon floor | Shot noise falls as 1/√n with n detected photons, so matching the quantisation noise of an E-bit converter needs about 3·4E photons per detection: about 200,000 (25 fJ at 1550 nm) at 8 bits. Each extra bit quadruples it. | [OptAI 02 · the photon floor](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_02_Optical_Matrix_Multiply/#slide-05); [OptAI 02 · interactive energy per MAC](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_02_Optical_Matrix_Multiply/#slide-06) |
| Conversion floor | The energy of the DACs and ADCs around an optical engine, each about Walden FoM × 2ENOB per sample and shared by N MACs. It, not the optics, usually dominates, and it sets the minimum energy any claim must cover. | [OptAI 02 · why converters dominate](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_02_Optical_Matrix_Multiply/#slide-07); [OptAI 04 · interactive claim checker](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_04_Landscape/#slide-07) |
| Static power per weight | Power drawn to hold a weight whether or not it computes, costing P/f per use at vector rate f: 1 mW at 1 GHz is 1 pJ per MAC, more than an 8-bit digital MAC at 45 nm. | [OptAI 02 · static power and utilisation](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_02_Optical_Matrix_Multiply/#slide-08); [OptAI 03 · interactive win map](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_03_Where_Optics_Wins/#slide-03) |
| Digital MAC energy | The yardstick: about 0.2 pJ for an 8-bit multiply and 0.03 pJ for an add at 45 nm, against 10 pJ for a 64-bit SRAM access and 1.3–2.6 nJ for a DRAM access (Horowitz). An H100 at peak dense FP8 is about 0.7 pJ per MAC at board level. | [OptAI 02 · the digital yardstick](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_02_Optical_Matrix_Multiply/#slide-11); [OptAI 03 · memory and Amdahl](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_03_Where_Optics_Wins/#slide-05) |
| Calibration and drift | Measuring and correcting each device's fabrication errors after manufacture, and holding the result against temperature. Self-configuring meshes tolerate split ratios from 85:15 to 15:85; heat moves every phase. | [OptAI 02 · calibration and drift](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_02_Optical_Matrix_Multiply/#slide-10); [OptAI 01 · coherent MZI meshes](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_01_Optical_Computing_Map/#slide-06) |
| Conditions for an optical win | Large matrices, low precision, static reused weights, low static power, high utilisation and weights held near the optics. Analyses place the photonic advantage at N > 500 and noise precision of 4 bits or fewer. | [OptAI 03 · the conditions for a win](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_03_Where_Optics_Wins/#slide-02); [OptAI 03 · interactive win map](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_03_Where_Optics_Wins/#slide-03) |
| Memory wall for optical compute | A DRAM access costs thousands of digital MACs, so an optical engine that makes MACs cheap but still fetches its weights saves little. Optical engines must be weight-stationary, and large models must be tiled and reloaded. | [OptAI 03 · memory and Amdahl](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_03_Where_Optics_Wins/#slide-05); [OptAI 02 · the digital yardstick](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_02_Optical_Matrix_Multiply/#slide-11) |
| 1/d scaling of optical energy | In an optical Transformer, optical energy per MAC falls as 1/d with model width d, because conversions and photons are shared by more MACs. Anderson et al. project about 100× for the largest current Transformers and over 8,000× at a quadrillion parameters (modelled, not measured). | [OptAI 03 · the scaling argument](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_03_Where_Optics_Wins/#slide-07) |
| Latency niches | Problems where the time for light to cross a chip matters: iterative solvers looping through one matrix (Ising problems at up to 1 GHz and 3 ns per cycle), control and signal processing, and sub-nanosecond classification. | [OptAI 03 · latency niches](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_03_Where_Optics_Wins/#slide-08); [OptAI 04 · Lightelligence](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_04_Landscape/#slide-03) |
| Optical AI scorecard | This series' judgement by workload: strongest for vision on light that arrives optically, plausible for CNN inference and iterative solvers, conditional on memory for LLM projections, poor for attention and hardest for training. | [OptAI 03 · a scorecard](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_03_Where_Optics_Wins/#slide-11) |
| Company claim | A performance figure stated by a company in a press release, product page or funding announcement. This series quotes, links and labels each one, and asks what operation, precision, power, baseline and workload it rests on. | [OptAI 04 · how to read a company's claim](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_04_Landscape/#slide-01); [OptAI 04 · questions to ask](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_04_Landscape/#slide-11) |
| Claim checker | Turning a claimed throughput and power into energy per MAC and comparing it with the conversion floor for an assumed array width and precision: a test of consistency, not truth, that shows what a claim must assume. | [OptAI 04 · interactive claim checker](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_04_Landscape/#slide-07) |
| Adaptive block floating point (ABFP) | A number format in which a block of values shares an exponent; Lightmatter's 2025 processor reports 16-bit ABFP operations computed with analogue photonic cores, reaching near-electronic precision on ResNet and BERT. | [OptAI 04 · Lightmatter](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_04_Landscape/#slide-02) |
| The pull towards interconnect | Companies that began in optical compute increasingly also sell optical interconnect, which moves bits digitally with no analogue precision to defend; Marvell's acquisition of Celestial AI's Photonic Fabric is the largest deal listed. | [OptAI 04 · the interconnect companies](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_04_Landscape/#slide-08); [OptAI 04 · the pull towards interconnect](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_04_Landscape/#slide-10) |
| Levels of evidence | From strongest to weakest: a peer-reviewed system running real models; peer-reviewed results on selected tasks; products announced with claims but no published method; funding with targets. | [OptAI 04 · what has been shown](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptAI_04_Landscape/#slide-09) |
